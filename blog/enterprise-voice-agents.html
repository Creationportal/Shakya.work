Why enterprise voice agents live or die at 400ms

Field notes from shipping 40+ production voice deployments.

The model is rarely the bottleneck in a voice agent. The call is. The moment a caller senses a delay — a half-second of dead air, a response that arrives a beat too late — their brain labels the system "a robot" and trust drops. Everything after that is damage control.

Latency is conversational, not technical

Humans take roughly 200ms to start a response in a live conversation. A voice agent running around 380ms end-to-end stays inside the window where turn-taking still feels natural. Push past 600–800ms and people start talking over it, the state machine gets confused, and the call degrades. That single number — round-trip latency — predicts adoption more reliably than any benchmark.

What actually eats the budget

In production the time goes to: ASR streaming, the LLM call (the biggest cost), TTS synthesis, and the network hop to telephony. The teams that hit 400ms aggressively stream partial results, pick a fast model for intent detection, and reserve the heavy model for synthesis only when needed.

The adoption test

We measure success not by accuracy but by whether a human operator stops double-checking the agent. When the floor stops listening in, the system has been adopted — and that almost always traces back to it feeling fast enough to be human.
